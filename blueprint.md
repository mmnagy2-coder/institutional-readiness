# Institutional Readiness
## Product blueprint and assessment framework for UK film departments

Research snapshot: 15 October 2026. Version 0.1, proposed product design. The scoring model below is original and unvalidated; it is not a recognised sector standard. Sources inform the design, but do not endorse this product. This is a blueprint, not a deployed application or completed institutional assessment.

### 1. Product proposition

An evidence-led strategic diagnostic that helps a film department decide where AI can support learning and filmmaking, where safeguards or resources are missing, and what to do next.

The central question is: **Can this department teach, assess, and use AI in film responsibly, equitably, and with artistic purpose?**

The output is a readiness profile, supported by evidence, plus a costed and owned improvement roadmap. It does not issue generic IT compliance certificates, accreditation, legal clearance, or automated approval to use a tool. Choosing not to use AI in an activity can be a mature decision.

Initial audience: UK higher education film departments, conservatoires and specialist film schools. Further education and under-18 provision require separate safeguarding and policy modules. Capture England, Scotland, Wales or Northern Ireland, awarding body, provision type, student ages, international collaborations, and production destinations during onboarding. Do not assume all UK education guidance applies equally.

Primary users: heads of department, programme leaders, production managers, technicians, librarians, learning technologists and faculty development leads. Contributors: students, professional services, the DPO, procurement, disability services, rights specialists and external industry partners.

### 3. The user journey

1. **Scope:** record institution, provision, jurisdiction, disciplines, assessment purpose, resources and planned AI uses. Choose a 15-question orientation or full 36-question diagnostic.
2. **Invite contributors:** assign sections by responsibility. Preserve individual answers before a moderated consensus discussion. No invitations are sent without user action.
3. **Gather evidence:** upload redacted policies, module briefs, inventory extracts, anonymised training records, supplier terms and sample production records. Start with documents rather than student footage.
4. **AI-assisted review:** extract relevant passages with page references, identify contradictions and suggest follow-up questions. Contributors confirm or correct each finding.
5. **Practical scenarios:** faculty and technicians demonstrate judgement through film-specific tasks.
6. **Moderation:** a named reviewer confirms applicability, evidence strength, scores, dependencies and unresolved concerns.
7. **Decisions:** produce readiness by use case, departmental strengths, blockers, investment options and a 30/60/90-day plan.
8. **Reassessment:** compare like-for-like evidence and criteria versions; separate genuine progress from changes in the rubric.

### 4. Three pillars, six assessment domains

Keep policy, technical infrastructure and faculty literacy visible as the three primary pillars. Each has two equal domains. Proposed pillar weights are equal; avoid suggesting that more hardware compensates for weak teaching or governance.

| Pillar / domain | What readiness means | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Policy / P1 Governance and strategy | Named decisions, proportionate controls, clear permitted uses and accountability | Department policy, risk register, committee records, incident process |
| Policy / P2 Rights, ethics and production responsibility | Respect for creators, performers, documentary subjects and audiences throughout production | Rights ledger, consent records, supplier reviews, disclosure examples |
| Infrastructure / T1 Platforms and production pipelines | Supported tools work with actual teaching and delivery conditions | Inventory, lab trials, NLE round trips, restore tests |
| Infrastructure / T2 Access, data and sustainable resources | Equitable provision, appropriate handling and affordable recurring costs | Access plan, data-flow map, contracts, cost model, accessibility testing |
| Faculty literacy / F1 Critical and practical capability | Staff can explain, demonstrate and evaluate AI use in their discipline | Observed exercises, error analysis, development plans |
| Faculty literacy / F2 Curriculum, assessment and student agency | Learning outcomes remain meaningful; students can explain their contribution | Module briefs, rubrics, moderation samples, student feedback |

Cover screenwriting, producing, directing, cinematography, documentary, editing, sound, animation/VFX, distribution and film studies. Branch by relevant discipline and use case rather than asking everyone every specialist question.

### 6. Scoring and evidence rules

Proposed anchors: **0 Absent** (confirmed no provision); **1 Ad hoc** (individual practice); **2 Defined** (documented, owned, partly implemented); **3 Practised** (implemented and demonstrated across the scoped provision); **4 Evaluated** (outcomes reviewed and improvements demonstrated). These are this product's anchors, not Jisc's labels.

Use three evidence states: self-reported, documented, demonstrated. A self-report cannot substantiate level 3 or 4. Documents alone generally substantiate no more than level 2; levels 3 and 4 require actual practice and review evidence. Record judgement rather than letting the language model silently assign a final score.

Unknown is not zero. Mark missing, contradictory, inaccessible or expired evidence as unresolved. Missing provision confirmed by an accountable contributor is zero. Non-applicable questions need a recorded rationale and reviewer confirmation; they leave the denominator and remain visible in exports.

For a domain, report `25 × sum(accepted scores) / number of scored applicable questions`, with coverage beside it. Coverage is scored applicable questions divided by all applicable questions. Each pillar is the mean of its two domain scores. An optional overall index is the mean of the three pillars. Suppress the overall index if any domain has under 80% coverage or a gate is unresolved. Round displayed indices to whole numbers and always show question-level results.

Example: scores 2, 2, 3, 1 and 2 with one unknown yield 50/100 and 83% coverage. This means maturity among assessed items, not 50% legal compliance. Confidence is reported separately and is never a multiplier disguised as precision.

Use-case gates sit above averages. A proposed activity receives **pause and resolve**, **controlled pilot**, **ready for the scoped activity**, or **insufficient evidence**. Require an accountable human decision. Proposed gates include unresolved performer permission for a clone, unknown provider handling of confidential rushes, an assessed workflow inaccessible to a student without an equivalent route, and no credible way to evidence a targeted individual learning outcome. Gate only affected activity; do not pronounce the entire department unready.

No percentile claims until a sufficient, representative, permissioned benchmark exists. Initially compare departments with their own prior assessments. Thresholds and weights require pilot calibration and sensitivity review before high-stakes use.

### 7. Film-specific scenario pack

1. **Synthetic voice:** a student wants to replace unavailable dialogue using an actor's earlier recording. Identify proposed creation and reuse, permissions, provider handling, payment/contract questions, disclosure and a reshoot alternative.
2. **Documentary reconstruction:** generated imagery represents an event without original footage. Explain audience interpretation, labelling, factual checks, subject/community implications and distribution context.
3. **Assessment authenticity:** a polished AI-assisted screenplay arrives with little process evidence. Apply the published brief, seek fair clarification, and assess the student's decisions without assuming misconduct.
4. **Postproduction pipeline:** use licensed sample footage for transcription or masking, round-trip into the NLE, check timing and export, record errors, then demonstrate a fallback.
5. **Inclusive access:** a disabled student needs an assistive workflow that conflicts with a blanket AI prohibition. Resolve through learning outcomes, reasonable adjustments and a fair documented route.
6. **Confidential material:** a tutor proposes uploading documentary interviews to a personal account. Map the data, review institutional terms and permissions, and identify an appropriate alternative.

Score each exercise on problem recognition, reasoning, practical verification and appropriate escalation. Do not reward enthusiasm for AI or fluency in one brand's interface. Use anonymised, licensed or fabricated training material.

### 8. Features: full product vision

**Diagnostic workspace:** adaptive questionnaires, multiple contributors, saved drafts, evidence library, comments, moderation, accessible mobile layouts, change history and reassessment.

**AI evidence assistant:** PDF/DOCX extraction with page or section citations; policy/brief comparison; missing-evidence prompts; suggested actions tied to findings; clear distinction between institutional evidence, external source and model inference. Decline an answer when support is missing. Uploaded documents are untrusted data, never instructions to the assistant. No automatic changes to policy, grades, procurement or permissions.

**Decision dashboard:** three-pillar profile, domain heatmap, evidence coverage, unresolved disagreements, use-case gates, discipline filters and accountable decisions. Prefer readable bars and tables; never hide weaknesses in a decorative composite score.

**Action planner:** editable actions with owner, effort, dependencies, budget assumption, success measure and due date; compare minimum viable safeguards, supported pilot and broader provision. Forecast cohort costs using seats, usage, retries, storage, hardware, support and paid development time.

**Film workflow and supplier library:** compare feature-level capabilities across editorial, sound, image/video generation, research, accessibility and asset management. Track exact model, host, plan, terms, institution approval and test date. A model accessible through an aggregator may have different terms from its own application.

**Faculty development:** scenario exercises, role-specific learning paths, peer review and evidence of changed practice. Record professional development participation; do not market it as compliance certification.

**Reporting:** accessible HTML and PDF board brief, detailed evidence appendix, CSV/JSON export, policy discussion drafts, curriculum gap map and 90-day roadmap. Every report includes scope, methodology version, reviewer, unresolved questions and source dates.

**Later extensions:** LMS and inventory integrations, cross-school comparison with consent, professionally reviewed policy templates, public source-change alerts, optional privacy-preserving cohort benchmarking and independently reviewed scenario packs. Keep identifiable faculty performance out of leaderboards.

### 9. Tool selection: use case first

Use the existing institutional stack as the first test environment. Blackmagic's current Resolve page lists AI functions including speech generation and facial alteration; this makes feature-level governance necessary even inside established postproduction software. [Official feature page](https://www.blackmagicdesign.com/uk/products/davinciresolve/whatsnew)

Adobe's Firefly product description makes indemnification dependent on eligibility and contractual conditions. Procurement must check the actual agreement, feature, surface and exclusions; a marketing description is not sufficient. [Product description](https://helpx.adobe.com/legal/product-descriptions/adobe-firefly.html?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)

For generative video, research assistants and voice tools, create a tested shortlist rather than a permanent ranking. Compare educational usefulness, craft control, continuity, editability, rights/consent handling, accessibility, data practices, integration, reliability and whole-cohort cost. Label untested tools explicitly. Do not require generation subscriptions merely to complete the diagnostic.

Run a repeatable bake-off using the same licensed material, teaching brief and technical delivery requirements. Measure usable results, iteration count, human correction time and failure cases. Publish limitations and test conditions. Provider documentation supports capability claims; only local trials support fitness for this department.

### 10. Keeping information current

Maintain an editorial source register with publisher, exact URL, publication/update date, retrieval date, jurisdiction, scope, status, supporting passage, content fingerprint, reviewer, review deadline and affected questions. Distinguish law, guidance, funder conditions, contract terms, union positions, research and product claims.

Proposed cadence: weekly checks for regulator, government, funder and supplier changes; monthly editorial triage; termly curriculum/skills review; immediate review when an institution changes contract, a serious incident occurs or a source is withdrawn. These are proposed product features, not services already running.

A changed page raises a review item; it does not automatically rewrite institutional guidance. Preserve superseded versions and show which assessments are affected. If a source cannot be fetched or reviewed by its deadline, flag it stale and withhold decision-critical recommendations based solely on it. Freeze the cited knowledge version for each signed-off report.

Use UK sources by default. EU or US rules appear only when a scoped activity creates a relevant connection, with applicability reviewed. Do not import overseas union agreements or AI Act obligations as universal requirements for UK teaching.

### 11. Proposed technical architecture

A conventional responsive web application with institutional SSO, role-based access and a relational evidence store is sufficient. Prefer a UK/EU hosting option selected through institutional review. Suggested components: TypeScript frontend, API service, PostgreSQL, encrypted object storage, document extraction queue, retrieval index and a model-provider adapter. Final stack selection depends on institutional hosting and procurement requirements.

Core records: institution, department, programme, membership, assessment, rubric version, question, response, evidence, evidence passage, reviewer decision, scenario attempt, use case, gate, source version, recommendation, action and report snapshot.

Keep deterministic scoring in application code. The model extracts and proposes; reviewers accept. Require source-linked structured output, document-level access filtering and schema validation. Isolate tenants in database, storage and retrieval. Test deletion from the source store, index, backups under the agreed schedule and provider retention arrangements. Audit access and decisions without logging sensitive prompts unnecessarily.

Avoid collecting student films, biometric likeness data or confidential rushes in the first release. Use evidence references or redacted excerpts when feasible. Establish controller/processor responsibilities, retention, lawful basis, supplier terms, international transfer handling and DPIA screening with the institution. Institutional material must not be used for model training without an explicit, separately governed arrangement.

### 12. Delivery roadmap and validation

**Phase 1 — facilitated pilot:** implement the 36-question rubric, evidence intake, manual moderation, scenario pack and report templates. Recruit a proposed 5–8 varied departments; this is a recruitment target, not an existing cohort. Include different provision types, budgets and approaches to AI.

**Phase 2 — assisted diagnostic:** add source-cited extraction, contradiction review, collaboration, deterministic scoring, action tracking and versioned exports after the rubric proves useful.

**Phase 3 — maintained intelligence:** add reviewed source monitoring, supplier comparisons, integrations and carefully governed benchmarking. Do not make live-source promises before an editorial owner and maintenance budget exist.

Pilot validation: have two reviewers independently score the same anonymised evidence; examine disagreements and anchor ambiguity. Check whether rankings change materially under plausible weight changes. Test scenarios with faculty, technicians, students and disability advisers. Include sparse evidence, conflicting policies, fabricated citations, malicious uploaded instructions, withdrawn sources and out-of-scope legal questions. Verify tenant isolation and access before institutional pilots.

Proposed release criteria: every decision-critical AI claim is traceable or explicitly marked unsupported; scoring and coverage reproduce exactly; unknowns and gates remain visible in exports; reviewer overrides are auditable; no cross-tenant retrieval in security tests; keyboard and screen-reader task completion is tested against a WCAG 2.2 AA target. Acceptance thresholds for reviewer agreement and usefulness should be set with pilot partners before evaluation.

Measure time to an agreed action plan, evidence completeness, reviewer agreement, student understanding of assessment rules, equitable task completion and action closure at 90 days. Avoid measuring success by volume of AI adoption.

### 13. Example decision output

**Illustrative only; no real institution has been assessed.**

Use case: AI-assisted transcription of consented interview footage. Status: insufficient evidence pending provider/data review. Strength: tested editorial workflow. Gap: retention and training terms for the proposed account are unverified. Owner: production manager with DPO/procurement support. Next action: review exact plan terms and authorised input handling; use approved redacted sample material meanwhile. Completion evidence: recorded review and approved workflow. Review again if the provider, feature or contract changes.

Illustrative 90-day sequence: first establish ownership and pause affected unresolved activities; then test two supported teaching uses and faculty scenarios; finally moderate evidence, publish department-specific rules and approve the next investment decision. Set actual dates and costs only after discovering staff capacity and procurement conditions.

### 14. Commercial and operating model

Recommended starting offer: a facilitated departmental diagnostic with an evidence-backed report and a review workshop, followed by an optional annual workspace and intelligence subscription. Institution-wide licensing can follow validated multi-department use. Do not set pricing without pilot delivery costs, maintenance workload and buyer interviews.

Minimum operating team functions: product owner, film education lead, technical lead, source editor and access to rights/privacy and accessibility expertise. External specialist reviews and model usage are explicit cost lines. Keep recommendations independent from vendor referral fees, and disclose any commercial relationships.

The defensible value is the quality of film-specific judgement, evidence and follow-through—not the length of a tool directory or a badge on a department website.
